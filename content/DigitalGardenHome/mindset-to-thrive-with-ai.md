# What mindset do humans need to thrive alongside AI?

What moves people to get up from the sofa? Decades of empirical research give a clear answer. Sustained intrinsic motivation requires three conditions: *autonomy* (action genuinely flows from one's own will), *competence* (the experience of being able to act effectively) and *relatedness* (embeddedness in meaningful relationships) (Deci & Ryan, 2000). Complementing these, contemporary positive psychology finds that human flourishing has five measurable dimensions: positive emotions, deep engagement in meaningful activity, high-quality relationships, a sense of meaning and belonging to something larger than oneself, and accomplishment (Seligman, 2011). These are not abstractions. They are the architecture of an entrepreneurial life design (Gedeon, 2024).

Enter *self-efficacy* — the belief in one's capacity to produce an effect in the world — as the source of volition. It is not a fixed trait. It is a nurtured and trained capacity, built through mastery experiences, role models, social endorsement and solved challenges (Bandura, 1997). It is the psychological foundation of entrepreneurship: the willingness to initiate, to take ownership, to co-create solutions rather than wait for instructions. *Given that in a fully AI-enabled economy each worker will have to assess the opportunities they could work on and opt for the one that offers the most promising value production, this mindset is not optional but essential for all workers.* Educational and organizational architecture in the Next Economy is therefore fundamentally an architecture of entrepreneurial mindset and practice.

Even more important than the *how* (entrepreneurship) is the *why*. The deepest motivational lever — what carries people through crisis — is not happiness or comfort but the experience that one's action is part of a larger context (Frankl, 1959). Crucially, this Why does not have to be discovered in isolation. It can be provided by an organization, a movement, or a shared vision of a desirable future — which is precisely what a compelling north star for the Next Economy aims to offer. The individual who joins a mission-driven enterprise, a regenerative community, or a peer-learning network finds their personal Why amplified by a collective one. People who hold a vivid image of a better future they can help build are systematically more motivated, more resilient and more creative than those who do not.

In network-society terms, the progression from *socialized mind* to *self-authoring mind* to *self-transforming mind* (Kegan, 1994, Illustration 1) is not a personal luxury but strategic societal infrastructure. A socialized self whose identity is anchored in external roles and expectations is systematically vulnerable to algorithmic manipulation and to Oblomovism. A self-authoring person has an internal value system and the metacognitive capacity to consciously choose which networks to participate in and which to resist. This is also the mindset of the entrepreneur and intrapreneur. The Next Economy needs this capacity mainstreamed as a cultural baseline, not as an elite trait, because in a fully AI-enabled economy, every employee and citizen benefits from embracing self-leadership, collaborative ownership and active participation in shaping the systems they inhabit.
