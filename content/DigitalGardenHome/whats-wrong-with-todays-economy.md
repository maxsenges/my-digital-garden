# What is wrong with today's economy, and why isn't it self-correcting?

We are not in denial that modernity delivered real progress. Life expectancy has risen dramatically. Extreme poverty has plummeted. Literacy, access to medicine and personal security have improved across almost every region of the world (Pinker, 2018). These results matter and should not be dismissed.

And yet the current Societal Operating System (SOS) is exhibiting structural failure modes — a decoupling of capital and networked technology from the people and communities they are supposed to serve:

1. Capital has become globally mobile at super-human speed and complexity, while social and ecological costs remain local and lasting.
2. Networked tech and social media platforms optimize for operator value, not participant flourishing.

Being human has, as a result, been progressively reclassified: from a subject living a life, to a "human resource," to an attention unit and behavioral prediction input.

This negative development was not fueled by individual greed. It is a governance gap: extraction is measurable, financeable and scalable in ways that human flourishing is not (yet). The Next Economy must correct the categorical error at the heart of modern economics: treating the wellbeing of individuals, the household of families and of society, as well as the "household" of our planet as externalities.

If managing an economy well means managing society's household — *Oikonomia* vs. *Chrematistics*, the maximization of profit for its own sake — then waste, fragility, addiction and depletion are not side effects, but operating failure.
