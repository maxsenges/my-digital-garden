# What resistance should I expect, and how do I address it?

The transformation is not frictionless. Three categories of resistance require direct strategy:

**Resistance from incumbents.** Organizations that have built competitive moats around opacity, lock-in, or proprietary standards will resist interoperability and transparency requirements. The counter-strategy is coalition: coalitions of enterprises, public institutions and civil society that demonstrate alternative models at sufficient scale to create switching costs for the incumbent. Demonstration beats argument.

**Regulatory inertia.** Existing regulation was written for a different economic structure. Regulatory sandboxes, mission-aligned procurement and algorithmic impact assessments are the primary levers for creating space for Next Economy business models without waiting for wholesale regulatory reform. Building alliances with regulators as partners — not adversaries — dramatically accelerates this.

**Geopolitical competition.** States and economic blocs that defect from humanistic norms capture short-term AI productivity advantage. The strategic response is not to defect in turn but to build the trust premiums and ecosystem partnerships that defection destroys. In a world of intensifying complexity, trust is the scarcest and least imitable resource. Long-term competitive advantage belongs to actors who compound trust, not extract it.
