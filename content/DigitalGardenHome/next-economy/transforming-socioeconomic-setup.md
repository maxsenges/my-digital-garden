# Part Two — How can we transform our socio-economic setup?

[[oblomov-effect|Part One]] argued *why* a Next Economy is desirable and *what* normative ground it stands on. Part Two turns to the harder question of *how* a society might coordinate itself to get there. The main organising logic we adopt is Mariana Mazzucato's mission economy (Mazzucato, 2021): rather than leaving transformation to the aggregation of market signals or reducing it to top-down industrial policy, a mission framework sets concrete, time-bound, outcome-oriented goals that are ambitious enough to require cross-sectoral mobilization among public institutions, private enterprises, research communities and civil society all pulling in the same direction. The Apollo "Moonshot" program is the canonical reference; more recent analogues include the EU's Horizon missions or the city of Amsterdam's mission to become a circular economy. Missions are neither forecasts nor subsidies; they are *shared public problems expressed as design briefs*, around which markets, research agendas and regulatory instruments reorganize themselves. For the Next Economy we propose four such missions:

1. [[mission-1-human-potential|Make human-potential development the primary "business" of society]];
2. [[mission-2-economic-architecture|Redesign economic architecture beyond GDP]];
3. [[mission-3-democracy-governance|Upgrade democracy and governance for the third millennium]];
4. [[mission-4-research-and-ai|Ensure research co-evolves with AI]].

Each is a grand challenge in its own right; taken together they describe the socio-economic setup a humanistic AI age requires.

The current document concentrates the depth of its treatment on Missions 1 and 2, with Missions 3 and 4 outlined as companion trajectories that can be developed in subsequent work. The asymmetry is deliberate. Mission 1, addressing human-potential development, or *Bildung* in the Humboldtian sense, is *the transformation engine* of the whole system. It provides the fertile ground on which flourishing becomes possible and the precondition for the other missions to succeed. Without a population equipped with agentive literacy, self-efficacy and entrepreneurial imagination, redesigned metrics remain abstractions, democratic upgrades misfire and AI-accelerated research outruns the human judgment that is supposed to steer it. Mission 2 deals with establishing the economics of the desirable future, is the second focal point because it is where the Next Economy actually "lives". If we cannot specify what we are optimizing for *instead of* GDP, and how value creation, investment, pricing and accounting are supposed to recognize stewardship of human and planetary flourishing, the Next Economy remains a slogan rather than a system. Missions 3 and 4 are no less important, as they shape the institutional scaffolding (democracy, governance, knowledge production) that keeps Missions 1 and 2 honest over time, but they are treated here as sketches, inviting the community to co-develop them.
