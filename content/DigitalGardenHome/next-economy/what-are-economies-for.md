# What are economies actually *for*?

Every economic system embeds an implicit answer to the question: *what counts as doing well?* Mainstream economics answers with GDP growth, productivity and preference satisfaction. The Next Economy requires a different foundational metric: what people actually become and do.

The capability approach (Sen, 1999; Nussbaum, 2011) provides a basis for exactly this. "Development as freedom" — Sen's framework, which earned him the Nobel Prize — proposes that the expansion of substantive freedoms to live lives of value, not merely formal rights or average income levels, are the most adequate measure of progress. A society is doing well when its members can develop and exercise their central human capabilities: enjoy good health, use their senses and imagination, experience deep emotion, exercise practical reason, form social bonds, shape their political environment and live in relation to the natural world.

This framework grounds the humanistic AI vision (Senges et al., 2025): the purpose of artificial intelligence is not to maximize platform engagement or shareholder returns, but to allow every person to unfold their potential — which in turn allows for the enterprises they work at to unfold their potential. An AI system that automates drudgery and returns time and energy to allow for more collaboration, empathy, entrepreneurship and imagination.
