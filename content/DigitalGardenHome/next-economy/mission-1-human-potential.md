# Mission 1 — How do we make human-potential development the primary "business" of society?

In a fully AI-enabled economy, the central productive work is no longer generating outputs. It is producing capable, self-directing humans. That sounds obvious, but most education systems still behave like credential factories designed for an industrial labor market.

The Next Economy requires a different key performance indicator: *Human Agency Capacity per capita*. This means designing learning environments and services that build self-efficacy, self-directed learning capacity and entrepreneurial competence as default mindsets and behaviors. The capability approach (Sen, 1999; Nussbaum, 2011) and the humanistic AI framework (Senges et al., 2025) provide the normative ground: *Bildung* (education as the lifelong cultivation of the whole person) is the institution most directly responsible for expanding what people can be and do.

## Concrete proof-of-concept models

- **School 42 Network** — tuition-free, peer-to-peer, project-based software engineering schools in over 30 countries, designed explicitly around learning-by-doing and learning-to-learn. No teachers, no lectures, no fixed curricula — only peer review and real deliverables.
- **Alemannenschule Wutöschingen** — German School Prize winner (2019, 2021). No fixed classes, no classrooms, no grades, no textbooks. "Learning companions" work with age-mixed groups; students manage their own learning journeys. First Abitur cohort (2022): average 1.7 vs. state average of 2.17.
- **Minerva University** — no campus, no lectures, rotating city immersion, small active-learning seminars. Trains transferable thinking and collaboration, not content delivery. Graduate placement consistently outperforms traditional peer institutions.
- **Olin College of Engineering** — integrated curriculum built around early hands-on engineering and real-world project work from the first semester, with explicit training in design, entrepreneurship and systems thinking.

The shared pattern is not pedagogical fashion. It is the operating model: learning is treated as production (projects), not consumption (lectures); learners practice autonomy, competence and collaboration as default behaviors; feedback loops are fast, social and anchored in real deliverables.

The market signal is unambiguous: the global education market stands at approximately $7 trillion and is growing at a 4.4% CAGR (HolonIQ, 2024), with edtech among its fastest-growing segments. The global coaching industry reached $6.25 billion in 2024, up 60% since 2019, and is projected to approach $9.5 billion by 2032 (ICF, 2025). Online coaching is growing at 13.2% annually, projected to reach $14.1 billion by 2033. 70% of Fortune 500 companies now use executive coaching, and companies with strong coaching cultures report 27% higher year-over-year revenue growth (BetterUp, 2023). UNESCO benchmarks public education investment at 4–6% of GDP or 15–20% of public expenditure. The mission is not only funding but *re-architecting what education produces*. A society where most people spend meaningful portions of their lives in self-motivated learning cycles — because learning is how one stays sovereign in an AI environment — will become more entrepreneurial, more productive and more democratic.
