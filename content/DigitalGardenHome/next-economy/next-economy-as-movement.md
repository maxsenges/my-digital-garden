# What makes the Next Economy a movement rather than a policy?

The Next Economy is not only a policy agenda or a business model. It is a transnational movement that integrates solidarity and sufficiency by growing a coalition of individuals, enterprises, communities and institutions that cross-promote and support each other's transformation — thereby generating synergies and momentum that no single actor could create alone.

Two wisdom traditions inspire the anthropological foundation. *Ubuntu* (South African for "I am because we are") anchors individual identity in community: not the isolation of the autonomous individual, but resonance with others is the place where human potentials genuinely unfold. And *Tikkun Olam*, the Hebrew concept of repairing the world, asserts that every person bears co-responsibility for the whole: the small actions of everyday life, summed, heal or break the world. Together they frame the deeper Why of the movement.

There is a hard strategic and economic logic here that complements the ethical one. Movements generate network effects. Organizations that participate in and actively support the Next Economy ecosystem gain access to a trust network and talent pipeline that isolated actors cannot replicate. This is the coopetition logic of the Next Economy: competing on differentiated value while cooperating on shared infrastructure, norms and narrative. Peer-production — genuinely collaborative value creation in which participants are not bound by hierarchy — has already demonstrated through open-source software, Wikipedia and Creative Commons that it can generate resources of extraordinary quality and scale (Weyl & Tang, 2023). The same logic applies to the economic and civic ecosystem of the Next Economy.

In the age of algorithmized connection, the quality of human community has become the decisive differentiating dimension. The deep experience that one's presence and contribution are genuinely desired — also known as *Belonging* — is not a soft nicety. It is a productive force. Organizations that design work as community and meaning-space generate stronger commitment, lower attrition and higher innovation. Movements that successfully weave belonging across organizational boundaries create the critical mass needed to tip systems toward new equilibria. The Next Economy is built one enterprise, one community, one deliberate beginning at a time, but those beginnings compound when they recognize each other as parts of the same direction of travel.
