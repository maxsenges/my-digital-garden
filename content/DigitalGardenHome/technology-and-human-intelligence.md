# How should technology relate to human intelligence?

We are living through an epochal transition. The institutions of print society (nation-state, market, university, public sphere) have not disappeared, but they survive rather than strive in a world that organizes itself by a different logic: fluid, decentralized networks in which knowledge, capital and agency are constantly reconfigured (Baecker, 2007, 2018; Castells, 1996, 2009).

A bet of the Next Economy is that this logic can be reconfigured. Open digital infrastructure enables a fundamentally different economy: decentralized social production in which commons can emerge that are economically non-rivalrous and socially transformative (Benkler, 2006). Doug Engelbart (1962) already articulated the right vision sixty years ago: technology as an *intelligence amplifier*, not a substitute for human judgment. Weyl and Tang (2023) extend this into governance: digital technology, properly designed, can vastly extend our capacity to collaborate across difference, with diverse communities retaining their distinctiveness while cooperating through interoperability rather than homogenization.
